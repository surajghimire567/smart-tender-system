Problem
Digitisation did not eliminate manipulation — it changed its form.

🔓 The 2026 e-GP breach (technical failure)

  • An insider with elevated system access exploited the fact that submitted bid rates were stored in readable, editable form before the official bid-opening event.
  • Rates were altered across 313 contracts using the credentials of 1,136 bidders.
  • Attack chain: external phishing via a counterfeit portal to harvest credentials + internal privilege abuse.
  • Critically, the system did not detect itself — the breach surfaced only through an external complaint.

🤝 The cartel case (non-technical manipulation)

  • A PPMO-commissioned report reviewed by CIJ Nepal documented contractors colluding with department officials.
  • Manipulation happened through legal discretion: tender packaging and qualification criteria written so only pre-selected firms could qualify.
  • Estimated loss to the state: Rs 8–14 billion.
  • No technical exploit was required — showing that system design alone cannot close every gap.

🧩 The structural gap this project targets

  • Bid data is not cryptographically protected until opening
  • Submissions are not immutable
  • System actions are not independently and continuously audited
  • Bidder identity is not strongly bound to a real person or device
  • No automated detection of suspicious bidding patterns

Publicly observable symptom: the platform allows no unauthenticated visibility into bidding activity, so oversight depends on insider trust rather than built-in verifiability.

Objectives
  1. Reverse-auction bidding platform — automate competitive ranking (lowest valid bid highest), removing a layer of manual, discretionary evaluation.
  1. Encrypted, insert-only bid storage — bids cannot be read or altered before the deadline, closing the readable-bid and editable-bid loopholes.
  1. Append-only, tamper-evident audit log — every action (tender creation, bid submission, award) recorded with timestamp and actor, so irregularity is system-detectable.
  1. Bidder-identity binding — device/IP fingerprinting at login and submission, against credential sharing and transferability.
  1. Rule-based anomaly detection — flags patterns such as repeated narrow undercutting between the same two vendors.
  1. Comparative evaluation — explicit mapping between documented real-world loopholes and the safeguards implemented here.
Scope

In scope

  • Full tender lifecycle prototype: creation and publishing (admin), vendor registration and bidding, reverse-auction ranking, award decision
  • Five safeguards, each mapped to a documented loophole: encrypted bid storage, insert-only submission with hash receipts, append-only audit logging, device/IP identity binding, rule-based collusion flagging
  • Demonstration environment (local or free-tier hosted) for academic evaluation and viva
  • Comparative case study against bolpatra.gov.np and the 2026 incident

Out of scope

  • Real government deployment, legal certification, PPMO integration
  • Multi-party key-splitting for bid sealing (single-key model used)
  • Hardware PKI tokens or MFA (device/session binding used instead)
  • Payment gateways, SMS notifications, document/KYC verification
  • Infrastructure hardening: penetration testing, code signing, network IDS
  • Institutional, legal and policy reforms from the cartel case
Limitations

🔑 Simplified cryptography

Standard symmetric encryption demonstrates the sealed-bid principle, but a privileged administrator could still technically reach the key — unlike a threshold, key-split production design.

🪪 Identity binding, not strong authentication

Device/IP fingerprinting reduces but does not eliminate credential sharing; it is not cryptographically verifiable identity the way PKI digital signatures would be.

🧱 No infrastructure-layer security testing

Security scope is the application layer only. Without penetration testing, code auditing or infrastructure hardening, the prototype cannot claim resistance to the server-level backdoor vector used in 2026.

📊 Rule-based, not learned, anomaly detection

Fixed statistical thresholds rather than a trained model; false positives and negatives are expected.

🧪 Academic-scale testing only

Untested at real transaction volume, government-scale concurrency, or under genuine adversarial conditions.

⚖️ Non-technical loopholes unaddressed

Cartel-style manipulation operates through legal discretion in packaging and qualification criteria. This is procedural and legal in nature and is explicitly outside the project's design scope.

Viva prep — test yourself

Why is a reverse auction appropriate for public tendering?

Procurement seeks the lowest acceptable price, so ranking is objectively computable from the bid value. Automating it removes a discretionary human evaluation step that was one of the manipulation surfaces in the real case.

If the admin holds the encryption key, what has actually been gained?

The threat model shifts: bids are no longer casually readable or silently editable by anyone with database access, and every access attempt is logged. Full protection would require multi-party key splitting, which is stated as a limitation rather than claimed.

What makes the audit log "tamper-evident" rather than just a log?

Entries are append-only and chained by hash, so removing or editing a past entry breaks verification of every later entry — the tampering itself becomes visible.

Why include a safeguard that admittedly cannot stop cartel behaviour?

Because the honest mapping — five technical loopholes closed, one procedural loophole out of reach — is itself a finding. It shows where system design ends and institutional reform must begin.

Build checklist

  • Tender creation and publishing (admin)
  • Vendor registration and login with device/IP binding
  • Encrypted, insert-only bid submission with hash receipt
  • Reverse-auction ranking and award decision
  • Append-only audit log across all actions
  • Rule-based anomaly and collusion flagging
  • Comparative case-study write-up and loophole mapping
